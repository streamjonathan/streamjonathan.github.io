Life is a surprise, from birth, to everyday, something new is always about to happen.

An acquaintance from Australia just arrived to stay with us for a few days, we are talking about life, work, plans, Jesus, the world. She met us when I spoke to a room of people about how I’m actually not good at doing anything related to Christianity, but how I am passionate about Jesus. Sounds strange, I know.

But life is strange. It’s full of hope and surprise. The impossible becoming possible. 
 I’ve shared before that most of what I own is pre-loved, and I think that’s good in a flashy world of purchases. But I’ve always assumed that my bank account status precludes some ventures in my life. Like starting a business. Not that I had anything specific in mind, but I thought opening a business could never be for me.

Then, last Sunday the doors of Cetaria opened. Without any investment, not a single dollar, I just launched a business. 
 Ok, actually my business partner started a shop and asked me to join him. 
 So how did that impossible surprise come about?

Instagram. 
 Ha, there’s a surprise! 
 With a friend’s old phone and a gift of a camera I started posting photos of food, wine, surfing, life. Here it’s impossible to miss the rolling lush hills and the briny blue sea, the magic simplicity of Basque food, drink and culture. Featuring all things beautiful around me, gradually followers flourished and interactions began. A comment popped up, an invitation to one of those free Instagram steak & cider banquets. You haven’t heard of them? I hadn’t either. But it was there that social media actually became social. All courtesy of Basque family businesses overflowing with culinary delight and…Instagram?

After that lunch, there was another outing, a wine-tasting, and a friendship began. We shared our dreams and our inspiration in Jesus. Where I didn’t see that I had anything to invest, my new friend, with eyes of faith, saw someone with whom to partner. Where I saw only water, my friend saw wine. The impossible turned possible. Surprisingly.

And that’s how an Instagram follower became a real friend and, finally, a business partner. Together we have shaped an empty storefront into a gourmet food and gift shop. Now that the painting and nailing is done, my days are brimming with meeting food and wine producers–and work is delicious sometimes! Txakoli is the local crisp wine whose tall vines almost overrun into our apartment. There’s the seafood (anxoas, my favourite) locally caught and processed (by neighbours!) into salty conserves. The art we display in the store depicts local scenes and the clothing from Brittany bears the white stripes of the sea. The once empty space is coming to life with the sea blue doors next to the tolling bells of the church building where Gipuzkoa was founded.

Cetaria. An impossibility made surprising reality.

Please do drop by our new store Cetaria, whether in person or from a distance. You can [like and leave a rating or comment on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/cetaria.eu) . A special thank you to the welcoming food & wine magicians known as the Basques and to all our dear friends and family who visit us or in some way walk along this journey, even if just in thoughts or prayer.

And, finally, my heartfelt thanks to the carpenter friend of fishermen who turned water to wine. Thank you for teaching me the value of good wine and nice food, but most importantly the immense value every person holds within.

-Jonathan
