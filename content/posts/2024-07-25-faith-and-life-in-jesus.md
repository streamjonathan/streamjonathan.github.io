As Jesus walks up to you, he invites you to follow. Repentance is a theological word, but sanctification might be even more helpful. From the point we take hold of what Jesus offers us, we are on a journey with him.

For those who may think repentance is a one-time experience, we know that many times the closest followers and friends of Jesus made many mistakes that brought them to a place of dryness, and they turned back to God. Repentance is an ongoing thing. It is not something we need to be afraid of, because the nature of God is so good and kind and loving that there is nothing to fear in God. Perfect love drives out all fear.

![Jonathan smiling in a car, holding a camera](/images/faith-and-life-in-jesus.jpg)

I think of Jesus stepping out of that stream of life that he is himself, that sustains all things from the very creation of the universe and time, and will go on for all eternity. He steps out and still invites us in, your story and mine, to follow.

One of the ways I have seen Jesus work in my own life is through dreams. People call that visions. Through scripture read out loud, through scripture meditated upon, through times of rest, and through times of great stress and anxiety, where God shows up in the most unlikely of circumstances. Through times of sadness, God is here.

If we embrace his presence, we go into that flow of love, that stream, that place of rejoicing. We find that our sin is washed away, our shame is taken away. Any guilt, any condemnation, it has all been consumed by the love of God.

So take the picture of Jesus walking through the stream of life. He is the source of all that life and light and goodness. He is walking up to you and asking you to follow him, to put down all the wrongdoing that has hurt others. He invites you to follow, to love, to let go of all those things that we are holding on to.
