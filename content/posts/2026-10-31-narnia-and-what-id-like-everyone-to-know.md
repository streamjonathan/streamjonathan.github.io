When I look back at my childhood, those books are key to my faith journey, following Jesus, understanding who Jesus is.

![Jonathan talking about Narnia in a green field](/images/narnia-what-id-like-everyone-to-know.jpg)

There is a magic in knowing God through a story, and then reading about what he is actually like in the scriptures, in Jesus. People who don't particularly like Lewis's portrayal of God as Aslan don't much like talking to me about God, inside Christianity or outside it. That says quite a lot. Lewis influenced me so much that I wanted to know who Jesus is after reading the Chronicles of Narnia.

Aslan is so good that it's impossible to portray him in a way that fully explains what Lewis was going for. Lewis did the best he could to explain who God is.

Narnia isn't an allegory. It's imagining an alternative circumstance — a world where Jesus is a lion, a world that is not fallen, where you could meet the first Adam and Eve. I did enjoy the BBC version. The French horn captures my imagination, my heart, every time I hear it. If you haven't read Lewis, try The Great Divorce, about heaven, and The Space Trilogy — the first two books are fantastic.

Watch the video: [Narnia and What I'd Like Everyone To Know on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U9BSyBTn6AY)

Will I watch the new film? I don't know if I'll laugh or cry or wonder. I'll probably be thinking of the first time I picked up The Magician's Nephew, a book that moved the hearts of so many people around the world. That's why it's loved so much. Sticking to the source material is always a great idea. It's up to you and me to discern and decide for ourselves what is true and right and good.

Keep on following Jesus. Look for him wherever you go and lift up other people. Peace to you.
