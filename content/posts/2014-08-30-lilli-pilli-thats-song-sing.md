*Memories of Lilli Pilli Primary School*

A brief two decades ago, in a coastal river town, we walked out beneath a forest of human hands, a farewell, off to high school.

A bright yellow and deep green memory with zippers that always stuck, grass stains from the out-of-bounds hill, and abrasions from the asphalt playground, its gravelly heat radiating.  In our small happy hands we held marbles, ninja turtles, troll dolls.  The girls’ hair longer than half their height, the boys attempting coolness with undercuts.

There were the first kisses, the dances, the romances, measured in days—a week was “serious” a month, next to marriage.

There were the punches in the face, a chair launched through the air, most of the blues resolved within a day, and we were best of friends again at play.

There were wide, roving playground games—cricket, releasings, hand ball, bull-rush, sconnings—everyone aspiring to be king or queen of the sports room.  English with Mr. Saundry.  Cricket with Mr. Paget—I still recall the crash and mercury flowing on a boiling day.  Beatles’ songs with Mr Mackleroy.  Maths and the “Triantiwontigongolope” with Mr Wotherspoon.  The scripture teacher exasperated to the point of tears.  Relaxation class with Mrs. Azzopardi—I could have laid there and slept for eternity.  Words are powerful, moments are important, some memories stay, others go.

The canteen scented of hot sausage rolls, euco’s, and carob buds, and after school the Gum Tree Store supplied our fill of slurpees, pencil ice blocks with chocolate nibs, and paper bags with 50 cents of lollies—enough for all your friends, or to make you sick.

Homework aside, we found copious amounts of time for bikes, swimming, and exploring our sun-filled world.  The river below, the odour of mangroves, the scuttle of soldier crabs and the unforgettable squawk of a cockatoo overhead.

We fought, we made up, we teased, we ran away, we gathered together.

Those were the sunlight-tinted years of childhood.  Not without their shadows, certainly, yet a time in life to which we look back as somewhat of a sanctuary before the changes, high school, the sorting hat, Caringbah or Woolooware, new friends, new adventures… Yet maybe we all would meet again, and recall those orange chair days with a gleam of something beautiful, long before, something to long for.

*Lilli Pilli. That’s the song we sing.* 
 *Lilii Pilli. Make the echoes ring.* 
 *We are proud to sing about the name,* 
 *We will al-ways try to bring it fame.* 
 *Lilli Pilli. Always striving high,* 
 *Standing every test;* 
 *May we always make our cry:* 
 *Lilli Pilli a-bove the rest!*
