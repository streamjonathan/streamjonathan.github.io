People often try to praise Jesus by calling Him a great moral teacher. On the surface, that sounds polite and respectful. Look closely at what He actually said, however, and that middle ground disappears.

In John 8:58, Jesus tells a crowd that knew His earthly family and hometown, "Before Abraham was, I am." In Hebrew and Greek, those two words — "I am" — echo the exact name God gave Moses at the burning bush in Exodus 3:14. Jesus did not say, "I existed before Abraham." He claimed timeless, self-existent being.

C.S. Lewis pointed out in Mere Christianity that a man who merely said the sort of things Jesus said would not be a great moral teacher — he would either be out of his mind or else something far greater. Either Jesus spoke the truth as God in human flesh, or His words collapse into delusion.

If Jesus is who He claimed to be, His teachings are not optional advice from an ancient sage. They are reality speaking directly to us.

When you read Jesus's words today, do you treat them as suggestions to consider, or as truth from the One who made you?
