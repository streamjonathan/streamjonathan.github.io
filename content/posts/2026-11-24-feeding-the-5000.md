Five thousand people. Five small barley loaves and two small fish.

A large crowd followed Jesus, because they saw the signs he had performed by healing the sick. Jesus went up on a mountainside with his disciples. Seeing the great crowd coming towards him, he said to Philip, "Where shall we buy bread for these people to eat?"

"It would take more than half a year's wages to buy enough bread for each one to have a bite!" Philip answered.

Andrew spoke up: "Here is a boy with five small barley loaves and two small fish, but how far will they go among so many?"

Jesus said, "Make the people sit down." There were about five thousand men. Jesus took the loaves, gave thanks, and gave them to those who were seated, as much as they wanted. He did the same with the fish.

When they had all had enough, he said, "Gather the pieces that are left over. Let nothing be wasted." They filled twelve baskets with the pieces left over.

After the people saw this sign, they said, "Surely this is the Prophet who is to come into the world."

Why did Andrew mention five loaves and two fish, when it seemed so little? And what does it say that nothing was wasted?
