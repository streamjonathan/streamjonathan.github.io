I made this one while playing Arc Raiders, with real people playing the game alongside me. I wanted to talk about faith, and about a book I love, while we played.

I cannot think of a better way to enjoy something than to share it. Just as Ratty and Mole found that the true value of the river lay in navigating it together, some of the added enjoyment of a story seems to be in the passing on of it.

![A lone figure walking across the ice in Arc Raiders](/images/arc-raiders-and-jesus-christ.jpg)

The book is The Great Divorce by C. S. Lewis. It is a story of two former co-workers meeting in the afterlife. One is a ghost from the shadows. The other is a man who committed murder and is now a solid person, standing in the light of the foothills of the new heavens and new earth. His task is to help his old acquaintance.

The ghost clings to his record of earthly decency. He is outraged at the sight of a redeemed sinner. It is a picture of human fairness colliding with divine mercy. And I think Lewis was on to something.

There is a line at the end of the book that has stayed with me for years. Lewis writes that heaven, once attained, will work backwards and turn even our agony into a glory. I love that. It means our suffering is not the end of the story.

I think about this when I think about what comes next. We are not going to be moved into some spaceless spiritual world. The plan for creation is a new heavens and a new earth. Emphasis on earth. Tangible. New. And our next body will be a renewed, glorified body.

That refreshes me. Even our aging and our suffering will be given a new perspective in eternity.
