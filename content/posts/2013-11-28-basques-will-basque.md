Daily life in a small coastal town in the Basque Country can be simple, uncomplicated—if    I let it.  Taste the fish, grilled with vegetables, oil, salt, simply adorned, deliciously devoured—that’s one way I can let life be.  Compared to what, you may ask?  Compared to life in an Australian city, a North American suburb or an Asian mega city, for example.  With my feet on these green hills and my board in the crashing sea, the *boinak* and *txakolin* bid me to live the Basque life.

With this simple life, comes a complicated language.  Our little family of three are Basque language learners (the two-year-old being far-and-away the best pupil!).  And, as travelers of this diverse earth we agree that no language is simple to learn, yet Basque and non-Basque *konporrak* alike often blurt out: “It’s a hard language!”  I wonder if that phrase, though kindly intended, doesn’t knock us off our feet before we even take a step.

A friend who learned this ancient language from scratch challenged us to keep two things in mind approaching this language marathon in which I am still an infant catching my balance: *motivation* is key, and *Basques speak Basque* .  Another friend summed up the two elements just the other day, when he said, “We’d rather you speak bad Basque than great Spanish!”.  I am trying to take their words of wisdom to heart, letting the pursuit of communicating with new friends in a unique culture overtake the hurdles of grammar, pronunciation and vocabulary.

In this race, I need those, like my encouraging friends, who will patiently endure my rigid, fumbling phrases and persist in replying to me in Basque.  English speakers in my homeland do this for English-learners, too, yet usually out of monolingual limits.  The Basques, however, could reply in French, English, or Spanish yet choose to speak from the heart.  In Basque.  As I study, regaining the childlike ability to learn without qualms of embarrassment, the Basque people play their key role as they forgive my butchered attempts to speak their heart language.  So, a cheer Gora! to all the patient Basques!
