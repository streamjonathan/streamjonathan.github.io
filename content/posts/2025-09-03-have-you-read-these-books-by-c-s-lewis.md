I know there are theological debates, and C. S. Lewis cops a lot of heat. His views show up in books like The Last Battle, where a person from outside Narnia walks through a doorway into the new heaven and earth, and Aslan greets him and welcomes him.

Lewis describes heaven as beautifully good, and hell as only kind of spatially in the blade of a grass. His conversations with George MacDonald in the foothills of heaven draw out what the reality of goodness is.

![Jonathan talking about the books of C S Lewis](/images/have-you-read-these-books-by-c-s-lewis.jpg)

Jesus said, blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. In John 14, my Father's house has many rooms, I am going there to prepare a place. God himself invites us into a physical, tangible place, not some far-off idea.

I want to share a personal story. When I first started to follow Jesus, I attended a Pentecostal leaning church and an Anglican church, the background C. S. Lewis comes from. Someone at a youth group said of the Anglican church, those people are just not really on fire. I knew something was drastically wrong. To look at someone else and see yourself as having a higher level. Jesus is the centre of what we are seeking.

I did not grow up in the church. I started reading the Chronicles of Narnia, and a friend gave me Lewis's theological writings. The Narnia books and the science fiction trilogy, especially Perelandra and The Great Divorce, have been very helpful to me. Our faith is not dependent on our performance. It rests on the Christ who died and rose again and empowers you to walk with him into that new heavens and earth.

I will finish with the end of Narnia, which will spoil the story if you have not read it. The kings and queens step into life eternal and realise Narnia had been just a glimmer of the true reality of who God is. Nothing good was ever lost. They walked up and further in, and were met with loved ones.
