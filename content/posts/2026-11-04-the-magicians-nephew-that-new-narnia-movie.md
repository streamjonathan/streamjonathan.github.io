When I look back at my childhood years, those books are key. They are key to my faith journey, following Jesus, understanding who Jesus is. The Magician's Nephew was the book I picked up first, and it moved me. It has moved the hearts of so many people around the world. That is why it is loved so much.

There is a new Narnia film coming, and people are talking about it. I don't know if I will laugh or cry or wonder when I watch it. I will probably be thinking of the first time I picked up The Magician's Nephew.

Here is what I think matters most. Narnia isn't an allegory. It is imagining an alternative circumstance. A world where Jesus is a lion. A world that is not fallen, where you could meet the first Adam and Eve. That is the magic of it. We encounter God through a story, and then we read about what he is actually like in the scriptures, in Jesus.

![Jonathan talking about The Magician's Nephew and the new Narnia film](/images/the-magicians-nephew-that-new-narnia-movie.jpg)

Aslan is so good that it is impossible to portray him in a way that fully explains what Lewis was going for. Lewis did the best he could to explain who God is. That is why I say, sticking to the source material is always a great idea. Will the film do that? I don't know. We are human, and we make our own decisions and express things however we want. It is up to you and me to discern and decide for ourselves what is true and right and good.

I did enjoy the BBC version, if you have time to watch it. The French horn captures my imagination and my heart every time I hear it. And if you haven't read Lewis, try The Great Divorce, about heaven, and the Space Trilogy. The first two books are fantastic.
