There are so many arguments happening, online and everywhere else. I wanted to share something I have been thinking about. If you follow Jesus, how you deal with criticism matters.

Choose your battles wisely. Make sure your heart and mind are ready before you engage, whether something was written to you personally or you just came across it. The way we respond to criticism can reflect love and open-hearted dialogue, or it can look like something else entirely.

![Jonathan talking about how we argue as followers of Jesus](/images/be-aware-of-how-we-argue.jpg)

I think one of the traps we all fall into is thinking that my group, my tribe, my part of the world, my experience means I have got it right. That is a danger whether you come from a Protestant, Catholic or Orthodox background. We can end up focusing on the outside things that make us feel right, instead of the movement of God's Spirit.

It reminds me of the story of the prodigal son. The father's love is abundant and open to both sons. One needs to turn back to God. The other is the brother who is very judgmental. The father's love is always accessible to both of them.

I am grateful for a lot in my own life. I grew up in Australia, in Sydney, in the nineties without the internet, with a big brother who helped me so much and loving parents who gave me everything I needed and more. Looking back, I can see how special those opportunities were. Life is not equal or the same for everyone.

So when I interact with people, I try to remember that everyone has a different experience, different pains, a different road they are travelling on. It is good to be aware of that, and to ask God to guide us in the words we speak.
