Middle Kingdom March 20, 2014 [Jonathan McCallum](http://www.jonathanmccallum.com/author/jonathanmccallum/) [Uncategorized](http://www.jonathanmccallum.com/category/uncategorized/) 0
I stood with my middle kingdom friends watching the sun set at the minority university, a wide open campus with basketballs and ping pong balls echoing around me. As an out-of-the-classroom language learner I was studying Mandarin behind a table tennis racket or leaping for a rebound. My most interesting basketball teammate, a Tibetan Lama, would hide the ball beneath his robe and roar with laughter.

From all over China, now gathered for an evening of English conversation practice, were faces as diverse as Middle Eastern-looking Kashgar Uyghurs, to broad smiling Tibetans to east coast Han Chinese.  Many heads turned as a Tibetan friend asked, “What is your religion, Jon?”.  Over steaming cups of yak butter tea I had become very fond of everything Tibetan, from religion to politics, so my words flowed from a place inside where Jesus had birthed spirituality.  I didn’t answer his question about religion, but talked about my Friend.  I couldn’t have guessed, but the students were intensely listening, without a theological word, no Christian concepts, just a person, a joy in knowing a person.

That evening I walked away with some new friends to find some ice-cream, realizing the sweetness of knowing “the” gospel. But since that’s not how we talk of friends, I call the gospel like it is: Jesus.
