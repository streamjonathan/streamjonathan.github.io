I hope my Protestant, Catholic and Orthodox friends watching this will consider the importance of story, and the way it can give us space to engage with these incredibly beautiful ideas about the nature of God. I ask myself, does my pursuit of God make me more hungry for the words and life of Jesus? I hope it does.

![Jonathan talking about Lewis and The Last Battle](/images/how-a-stable-holds-more-than-we-could-ever-imagine.jpg)

My name is Jonathan. Jesus is the person who has changed my life, and the story of Aslan in The Chronicles of Narnia was profoundly important to me, because that was my first experience of the nature of God. I did not grow up in a religious family, and I thank God for how story gave me space to understand God, and to thirst for more.

Aslan, especially in The Last Battle, has a particular influence on the way I see God and the world and eternity. There is a scene where Lucy and Digory and Tirian face a difficult decision, how they are going to fight to the end bravely against their enemies.

There is a stable in the story. Inside that stable there is actually something much more. For those who have bad intentions, what is in the stable is their worst nightmare. For those who have a desire to know God, what happens in that place is very profound.

Lucy says this, after she notices that what is inside the stable is much bigger than what was seen from outside. In our world too, a stable once had something inside it that was bigger than the whole world. There is something about Jesus that is bigger than our entire world, bigger than our existence, bigger than our universe, and yet he was born in a stable.

Imagine yourself in that battle, where Lucy discovers the joy of her heart inside what others found to be a terrible, blinding situation. They were unable to see the immense eternity inside that stable. I hope you do not miss it.
