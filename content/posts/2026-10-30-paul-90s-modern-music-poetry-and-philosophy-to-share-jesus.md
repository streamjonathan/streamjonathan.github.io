Paul addresses this by doing the exact opposite of what you might expect. When he spoke to the Athenians, he used their poetry and their language, the way they understood God. And he spoke about Jesus as the centre of creation, the one who holds everything together.

Paul doesn't have an idea that everything is bad. He also doesn't think that evil and bad don't exist. He challenges the worldview of the time, and he does it by starting where people already are.

This is about 90s music. If you are my age, you will remember people from religious circles saying this music is bad or unhelpful. I didn't even grow up in a religious household, but I noticed the trend.

![Jonathan talking about Paul, 90s music and poetry](/images/paul-90s-modern-music-poetry-and-philosophy-to-share-jesus.jpg)

When God creates life and culture and places and people, he creates this to be good. There is a disconnect and a breaking of relationship in the story of creation, and a brokenness in our own hearts. But it doesn't mean that everything is just bad.

I think it is a really interesting discussion to get into, related to music. With all things, there is a need for balance and love. Paul really sums it up very well. The focus is upon Jesus, and love for other people, including others who are in the faith. If someone has a problem with a specific song, maybe don't play the song around them. That is a very simplified version of what I think Paul was saying. Treat others with so much respect that you care for their very wellbeing.

Jesus is the healing medicine, the remedy for this. Jesus is the way that we find life. One of the verses that comes to my mind is from the start of John. God was there at the start. The one who knows us, and the one who creates all things.
