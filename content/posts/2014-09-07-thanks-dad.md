Thanks, Dad September 7, 2014 [Jonathan McCallum](http://www.jonathanmccallum.com/author/jonathanmccallum/) [Jonathan](http://www.jonathanmccallum.com/category/jonathan/) 0
[![](/images/2014-09-FathersDay.gif)](http://www.jonathanmccallum.com/2014/09/07/thanks-dad/fathersday/)
Today is Father’s Day in Australia. As the thunder drummed and lightening flashed, I decided to go surfing. The ocean was green, pristine, still.  The sky gray, dark, then almost neon green. I felt like I was flying along a silver emerald world I had never seen before.  I couldn’t stop smiling, even singing.  Back at home I wanted to express thankfulness to those around me, as well as those I have enjoyed this amazing world with who live across the sea.

Thanks, Dad, for your laugh, how you listen, for teaching me to love the surf, how to box, and to love to read. You taught me to respect, to run from greed, to live life generously openly, like a friend. You just laughed when I shook up your beer and didn’t get angry when I accidentally flung that dart into your big toe, you just said: “Maybe no more darts for a while.”

Thanks, Mum, too, because you are you, your sensitive heart, seeing beyond. You taught me how to be nourished by your skill in the kitchen, to speak up when needed, to communicate what’s inside, not to hide, to see beyond text book education, to value people on every occasion.

Thank you, Dad & Mum, for giving me the joy of traveling as I grew up, exploring the UK for two years—parks, green lands, castles, my imagination re-birthed for all things of a distant misty castle age—and the trips to the islands, surfing those crystal clear blue waves, oh those wonderful Honolulu days.

Thanks to my brother, and a dad as well. Your wit and especially your humour I carry along the way, the summers we spent outdoors making G.I. Joe & Star Wars bases in the sand-mountain out back, the winter nights sharing the heater, cricket days, pool drippy evenings and jumping in the ocean.  Plenty of wonderful days together with neighbours and friends.

As the light fades on the green hills here and the sun rises there on the cool Sydney shore, I see the stories merge and blend like the colors of the storm on the sea, and I am thankful for every shade.
