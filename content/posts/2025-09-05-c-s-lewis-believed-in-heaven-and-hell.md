C. S. Lewis does a great job of condensing big ideas into a story. The Great Divorce is one of his best books, in my opinion. He talks about heaven not in terms of what it would look like, but in terms of the decisions we make each and every day.

I think the idea of heaven is often reduced to one side of a coin, heaven on one side and hell on the other. I think that is a spatial and philosophical error. It makes you start to consider the hugeness of the goodness of God, and how small and reduced it is to be separated from God by our own decisions.

![Jonathan talking about C S Lewis and The Great Divorce](/images/c-s-lewis-believed-in-heaven-and-hell.jpg)

It helps me to think of hell not as the opposite of heaven, but as a place of ultimate free choice, to be away and separated from God. There are big questions here that I will not go into, like why God would give us the opportunity to choose to be separated from his goodness. I want to at least acknowledge that those questions matter.

In John 14 Jesus says there are many rooms in my Father's house, and he is going to prepare a place for each of us, and he will come back and take us with him. We know he goes to the cross, he dies, he rises again, and that shows us the way toward eternity with him.

Turning to God, repentance, simply means turning and changing our mind. It has a lot to do with eternity in heaven. I believe God places his Spirit in us as we say, Jesus, have your way. Walk into my life. You are the way and I want to follow you.

Look at yourself in the light of what scripture teaches about who Jesus is. Look at the people around you whose lives overflow with joy and peace and patience and love and self-control. That fellowship we have inside the body of Christ helps us see our walk with God more clearly.
