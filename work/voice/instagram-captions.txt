cascade path of my soul by @jonamccallum

The soft morning light melts as buttery orange drizzling sun in a blue sky pancake. 

An ocean north, the hills south, we find ourselves packed into a cozy kitchen.

A brief cheeky prayer lifted towards a heavenly parent from a hungry child's mouth, the ocean spray breath holding seagulls above the ageing apartment block. 

A field of sheep chewing happily away on a lush earthy bearded hillside, a yawning dad commenting on the aromas within and flowing from the lives around the wooden table. Sleepy dusted eyelids of cherished ones. 

A few chirpy sparrows alighted on the window sill to see a pan sizzle-popping eggs and thin sliced bacon, toast spread generously (some may say greedily) with sweet fruits once squashed and squelched into jars. 

A prayer in a thought of "Thank goodness Himself" said in loud, silent love syllables. All of us together, looking toward forever on this beautiful day's eve. 

Eyebrows raise as we gaze out on the mist hugging the trees, horses and sheep, our souls soak in a daily bread of sorts and the burden of yesterday's mistakes carried and lifted away by a Heavenly Father. 

A breakfast, and quite possibly, a glimmer-glimpse of forever.

Water can dance over river rapids, or freeze still over slow moving bends in the creek. I have splashed and cracked my way, venturing through both streams and icy water. The memories linger with me and rest like snow crystallising inside my soul.

Salty ocean bays call pelicans from afar to their waters to scoop out darting schools of fish, a delicious meal for a bird that travels the oceans with ease. I often retell my fishing stories to the kids around the dinner table--we would wade into the shallows, sliding our feet to push along the sting rays looking up at us, and grab a blue swimmer crab before it scuttle-swam into deeper sea beds.

Deep within I long to know more more of Life. It seems brief, taking us along for the ride through a bunch of solar spins then casting us out into the unknown place where many, in our quiet moments, approach with trembling. I know I tremble at the thought of the hereafter. Yet I also find peace in knowing that this life is like the water of the river and the tides, and I am carried along to where I do not know I am going. To write is to force myself to do what is uncomfortable, to live Life open-hearted and let go of fear. Fear of living and fear of passing.

I find religion tries to sum things up concisely, often falling far short of the mystery and wonder I see in both creation and Creator. In the carrying along as I am rushed along over the golden sandy river bed, I learn to lay back and look up at the sky that turns to stars, the autumn leaves fall into the water and the sense of trust I know is possible almost seems attainable. I hear a voice sing out to me, I hear the liquid laughing song along the shore--or is it from the earth or sky? It even seems to dwell in me, that music. What Is Parkinson’s Disease?
Parkinson’s disease (PD) occurs when brain cells that make dopamine, a chemical that coordinates movement, stop working or die. Because PD can cause tremor, slowness, stiffness, and walking and balance problems, it is called a “movement disorder.” But constipation, depression, memory problems and other non-movement symptoms also can be part of Parkinson’s. PD is a lifelong and progressive disease, which means that symptoms slowly worsen over time.

The experience of living with Parkinson’s over the course of a lifetime is unique to each person. As symptoms and progression vary from person to person, neither you nor your doctor can predict which symptoms you will get, when you will get them or how severe they will be. Even though broad paths of similarity are observed among individuals with PD as the disease progresses, there is no guarantee you will experience what you see in others.

Estimates suggest that Parkinson’s affects nearly 1 million people in the United States and more than 6 million people worldwide.

This is NOT a prediction if I will, or if you will, like or appreciate the movie. I'm just happy we are talking about faith in Jesus because of these books by Lewis.

Greta Gerwig's Narnia: The Magician's Nephew is coming — in cinemas Feb 2027, then Netflix in April. I made a video about the new film, Lewis and the nature of God. You can watch it on my YouTube — link in my bio.

When I look back at my childhood, those books are key. They're key to my faith journey, following Jesus, understanding who Jesus is.

I've found that people who don't particularly like C.S. Lewis's portrayal of God in the form of Aslan don't particularly like talking to me about God. And it says a lot that Lewis influenced me so much that I wanted to know who Jesus is after reading The Chronicles of Narnia. The magic of knowing God — that we could somehow encounter him through a story, and then read about what he is actually like in the scriptures, in Jesus.

My thought is that Aslan is so good it's impossible to portray him in a way that completely explains what Lewis was going for. Lewis himself did the best he could to explain who God is.

The Chronicles of Narnia aren't actually allegories. They're imagining an alternative circumstance — a world where Jesus is a lion. I did enjoy the BBC version, if you have time to watch it. The French horn captures my imagination, my heart every time I hear it. And if you haven't read Lewis, I recommend The Great Divorce, which is about heaven, and The Space Trilogy — the first two books are fantastic.

I think any movie trying to adapt Narnia will do a good or a bad job. That's not really my interest. Will I watch the movie? I don't know. I'll probably be thinking of the first time I picked up The Magician's Nephew, a work of literature that moved the hearts of many people around the world. That's why they're loved so much. Sticking to the source material is always a great idea.

It's up to you and me to discern and decide for ourselves what is true and right and good. Have a great day. Keep on following Jesus. Look for him wherever you go and lift up other people.
