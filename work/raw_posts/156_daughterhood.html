Daughterhood

By Jonathan October 4, 2016 Jesus
  • No Comments
  • 0
  • 0
    0

As a parent of two young growing women, there is nothing more rewarding in life than having this lifelong job of raising and encouraging them. No one could afford to pay me or my wife for this job–it is just too valuable to put into monetary terms. But the parenting role is not always compensated in our society just as women’s role is not always valued. So my daughters are raised in a world with unfair rules and unfair distribution of wealth and privilege–in some ways to their benefit and in some ways not. What does Jesus speak into their and other women’s lives?

Well, he refers to himself as a mother hen, saying, “I have longed to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings.” And all that we know about Jesus shows us more of God. Jesus gathered kids to himself, scolded the adult male disciples for trying to stop him hanging out with children, with other female disciples to be, welcomed outcast women into the circles of religious men, pointing to their faith as the example while rebuffing the theological deceit of men.

In his Kingdom, the first will be last and the last first. It’s an upside-down Kingdom with not one gender above the other. A Kingdom of love. And love is the only power that will last and reach beyond any control or unquestioned cultural forms that restrict and restrain and diminish who we are and how we are all, both genders made in the image of God.

Jesus told the story of the Loving Parent, portraying the parental nature of God. In a retelling below I have shared it inclusive of mothers and daughters. If this ruffles your theological feathers try not to peck, or cage, or tell another soul that was born to fly to stay down while you soar in the breeze. Learn to fly with me, and embrace life, be filled with joy and value both genders that Jesus created.

Once there was a young woman who asked her parents for her inheritance. She left home without looking back, and her parents held one another as they watched her go, her older sister standing by with folded arms.

Life was exuberantly and lavishly lived in a foreign land. Until the inheritance money ran out. She did not yet know that her true inheritance–daughterhood–would never cease. Her parents’ love could not run dry like money and it could never be wasted.

She decided to begin the journey home. She was hungry and she wept as she walked, but sunshine melted from the blue sky, soaking courage in her and a clear stillness settled as she heard a Parent, like a Spirit or like a Kingly Son say, “I’m here.” After days, a warmth of solar shine and a sweet sea breeze wafted over her, the salt that refreshed her corroding the steel weapons of the guards she passed at the city gates. The sand of the bay sparkled at low tide as gulls caught updrafts, boats bobbed and her heart crackled and leaped with a hope’s flame.

Then, from a distance she saw her mother running toward her, her father not far behind as he hiked up his robe too. They both embraced her with lavish kisses, abundant hugs, and then the party began. Never has a feast nor dance been so joy-filled.

But there was one person who was upset; “Why this waste?” the older daughter fumed. “She has already wasted all your inheritance and I have worked so hard for you!” Her mother embraced her and simply said, “We would do this for you.”

(Based on the story Jesus told of the “Forgiving Father” also known as the “Prodigal Son”)

-Jonathan McCallum
